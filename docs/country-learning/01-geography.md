# 1. Geography

Help users understand where a country is, what its physical environment is like, and how location shapes settlement, resources and connections.

## Categories to learn

- **Location and extent:** neighbouring countries, seas, islands, major geographic regions and distance.
- **Landforms:** mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys and coastlines.
- **Water systems:** rivers, lakes, catchments and groundwater where documented.
- **Climate:** regional patterns, seasons, long-term averages and variability.
- **Resources and land use:** agriculture, minerals, forests, protected areas and urban land.
- **Environment:** ecosystems, conservation and dated environmental pressures or hazard studies.

Administrative, physical and ecological regions are different ways to describe the same territory. Make their purposes clear.

## Interactive learning

| User activity                                 | App accommodation                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Locate a country and explore its surroundings | Focus the country, show geographic context and allow boundary/source inspection    |
| Click a region, river or protected area       | Select the verified feature and open a closable explanation with its source        |
| Compare two environments                      | Use a split map/chart with equivalent measures and periods                         |
| Ask how landscape relates to settlement       | Show relevant geographic and settlement evidence; explain documented relationships |
| Explore change                                | Select a dated land-use series or environmental timeline when available            |

Offer a short introduction first, with deeper terms such as catchment or biome explained on demand. Use a map when spatial context matters and an accessible table when comparing measurements.

## AI-orchestrated example

**Question:** "Show the major river systems and explain how they relate to settlement."

AI checks whether reviewed river geometry and compatible settlement evidence exist for the selected country. If supported, it enables the appropriate layers, focuses a manageable area and explains the documented patterns with citations. A feature click becomes context for "Where does this river flow?" or "Which places are connected to it?"

If only country and administrative boundaries exist, AI states that river and settlement layers are unavailable and offers the reviewed geographic overview. It must not draw plausible rivers or infer water supply from proximity.

## Evidence and integration

Useful source families include national mapping agencies, environmental and geological agencies, meteorological services, documented spatial datasets and reviewed geographic research. Retain geometry precision, original feature IDs, collection or observation dates, coordinate systems and licences.

Add physical-feature records and topic tags linked to stable geography IDs. Terrain, climate, land-use and hazard layers need specific sources, renderer support and registered capabilities. A historical climate average is different from a weather forecast; a hazard study is different from a current alert.

Show boundary publisher and edition. Different providers may simplify coastlines differently or represent disputed areas differently; preserve the source context.

**Current foundation:** country boundaries, dated reference areas, South African municipal/ward boundaries, geographic focus and online street detail. Physical/environmental learning layers and broad geographic explanations require additional reviewed content and implementation.

## Learning outcome and completion check

The user can locate a place, distinguish physical and administrative geography, and explain one sourced geographic pattern. Verify that selecting a feature opens the same identity in details, every enabled layer has appropriate attribution, and unsupported geography produces a clear alternative.
