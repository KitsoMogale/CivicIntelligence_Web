# 4. How a country is divided

Administrative divisions are the areas used to organize a country and local government. Voting, statistics and traditional leadership may use different areas.

## What users can learn

- **Main areas:** states, provinces, regions or the country's own equivalent.
- **Local government:** councils, municipalities, towns and villages.
- **Voting areas:** constituencies, wards and polling districts.
- **Statistical areas:** areas used to count people and publish figures.
- **Traditional or special areas:** recognized local arrangements and their roles.
- **Boundary changes:** splits, mergers, renamed areas and older versions.

Two areas with the same name may have different boundaries or purposes. Do not force all these systems into one tree.

## How users can explore

| User wants to            | The app can show                                         |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Understand the divisions | A choice of area types, with local names and dates       |
| Select an area           | Its map shape, purpose, source and details               |
| Explore smaller areas    | Available child areas and a path back to the larger area |
| Compare systems or dates | Separate layers or side-by-side views                    |

Keep official codes, numbering gaps and older IDs. Do not add missing numbers just to make a list look complete.

## Example with AI

**Question:** "Show local councils and explain how they differ from voting areas."

AI checks which systems and map versions are available. It uses the country's own names and explains the purpose of each type of area.

If the app only has old district maps, AI must not label them as current council boundaries. Botswana's older reference districts, local councils and voting areas need separate treatment. Namibia's regions, constituencies, local authorities and traditional arrangements also need separate treatment.

## Information and app changes needed

Use official boundary bodies, local-government records, election bodies and statistics offices. Keep original codes, area types, dates, sources, map versions and reuse terms.

Build support for each country's systems, including areas that overlap. Add checked links between statistical and government areas when needed.

An area on a reference map does not by itself prove today's legal boundary or government powers. Keep the known date problem in the Namibia source visible wherever those maps are used.

## What already works

There are 237 country/area profiles and dated smaller-area maps for 214 of them. South Africa has municipal and ward support; Botswana and Namibia have initial descriptions of their systems. Many countries still need detailed local and voting-area maps.

## How to check it

Users should know what an area is for, which source it uses and its map date. Check local names, larger/smaller-area links, date notes and whether saved views restore the correct boundary version.
