# 3. Government

Help users understand how public decisions are made, what institutions do, and how authority, accountability and representation work in the selected country.

## Categories to learn

- **Constitutional system:** constitutional foundations and the country's institutional form.
- **Executive:** head-of-state/government roles, cabinet, ministries, departments and agencies.
- **Legislature:** chambers, representation, legislative powers and scrutiny.
- **Judiciary and oversight:** courts, judicial independence and oversight institutions.
- **Elections and participation:** electoral systems, political parties, voting and public participation.
- **Leadership over time:** offices, appointments and officeholders when supported by dated official evidence.

Separate an office from its incumbent. Describe each country's constitutional arrangements using its own terminology.

## Interactive learning

| User activity                                 | App accommodation                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ask how government is organized               | Expandable institutional structure with typed connections                 |
| Click an institution                          | Show its role, powers, jurisdiction, sources and documented relationships |
| Ask who checks a decision-maker               | Focus a bounded relationship graph of oversight and accountability        |
| Follow a public decision                      | A sourced process flow, such as the documented stages of a bill           |
| Explore changes in leadership or institutions | Dated events and terms, with evidence and explicit coverage limits        |

Use hierarchy only for actual containment or reporting relationships. Independent institutions and distinct spheres of government may require grouped diagrams and labelled links.

## AI-orchestrated example

**Question:** "Who makes laws, and who can review them?"

AI retrieves the selected country's constitutional and institutional evidence, resolves the relevant bodies, and chooses an appropriate structure or process view. It distinguishes legislative, executive, judicial and advisory roles rather than reusing one global government template.

The explanation links to the exact institutional sources. Selecting a body supports follow-ups such as "How are its members chosen?" or "Show its documented oversight relationships."

If current membership or officeholder data is absent, explain the institution and disclose that limitation. Do not generate a current leader list from model memory.

## Evidence and integration

Use constitutions, official institutional mandates, parliamentary and court publications, electoral authorities and dated appointment/election records. Preserve effective dates and distinguish legal powers from how a source describes their exercise in practice.

Represent offices, institutions, persons and mandates separately as coverage expands. Relationships need precise meanings: appoints, reviews, regulates, advises or reports_to are not interchangeable. Directory links such as includes support navigation and do not establish authority.

A process diagram needs sourced stages and exceptions. An AI explanation should not turn a broad civics summary into a definitive account of every legal procedure.

**Current foundation:** South African institutional records, initial Botswana/Namibia profiles, hierarchy/relationship views and source details. Comprehensive country structures, current officeholders, election datasets and most legislative processes require further curation and capability work.

## Learning outcome and completion check

The user can identify major institutional roles and explain one documented accountability relationship. Verify arrow meanings, independent-body representation, source dates and country isolation. An uncatalogued relationship should be described as unavailable, not nonexistent.
