# 3. Government

Help users understand who makes public decisions, what government bodies do and how they are held responsible.

## What users can learn

- **The country's system:** the constitution and main government arrangements.
- **Executive:** the people and bodies that run government, such as the president, cabinet and ministries.
- **Legislature:** the body or bodies that make laws.
- **Courts and oversight:** bodies that interpret laws, review decisions and check public power.
- **Elections:** how representatives are chosen and how people take part.
- **Leadership changes:** who held an office and when, where checked records exist.

Keep a role separate from the person holding it. Use each country's own names and arrangements.

## How users can explore

| User wants to                     | The app can show                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Understand the structure          | An organization diagram with expandable parts        |
| Select a public body              | Its role, responsibilities, area of work and sources |
| Ask who checks a decision         | A graph of the documented links                      |
| Follow a law or leadership change | A process diagram or dated timeline                  |

Only use a top-to-bottom diagram where that structure is real. Independent courts and separate levels of government must not look like departments taking orders from one another.

## Example with AI

**Question:** "Who makes laws, and who can review them?"

AI finds the country's checked constitutional and government information. It selects the right bodies, shows a useful diagram and explains their different roles.

Clicking a body can lead to "How are its members chosen?" or "Who checks its decisions?" If current members or leaders are not recorded, AI says so rather than guessing from memory.

## Information and app changes needed

Use constitutions, official descriptions of powers, parliament and court publications, election bodies and dated appointment records.

Save organizations, offices, people and responsibilities separately. Explain each connection: appoints, advises, regulates, reviews or reports to. A link that simply groups entries must not look like a line of authority.

Processes need sources for their steps and exceptions. A short explanation should not pretend to cover every legal detail.

## What already works

The app has South African government entries, initial Botswana and Namibia profiles, organization diagrams, connection graphs and sources. Complete structures, current leaders, elections and most lawmaking processes need more checked information and features.

## How to check it

Users should be able to explain the main roles and one documented link between them. Check arrows, independent bodies, dates and the selected country. A missing saved connection does not prove no real-world connection exists.
